The Virtuous Path to Elysium

A Modern Revival of the Cultus Deorum Romanorum & the Hellenic Sacred Tradition

The Four Goals of Human Life

A righteous life is built upon the pursuit of four harmonious ends. Neglecting any one invites imbalance, while fulfilling all four prepares the soul for ultimate peace.

1. Live Virtuously

The sovereign foundation. Living by the 21 sacred virtues in thoughts, speech, and deeds ensures that an individual remains fundamentally righteous before the gods and mankind.

2. Embrace Pleasure

Sensory joy, great food, affection, and aesthetic beauty are divine gifts to be savored. Enjoy desire freely without slipping into decadence or compromising moral virtue.

3. Cultivate Prosperity

Ensure your family thrives without want, contribute meaningfully to civilization, and provide the next generation with the stability to lead virtuous lives.

4. Attain Salvation

The ultimate culmination: preparing the soul for the Elysian Fields through a life of righteous weight, steering clear of the purifications of Tartarus.

Judgement, Reincarnation & The Afterlife

Across ancient Rome, Greece, Egypt, and Zoroastrian Persia, ancient records attest that the soul meets three divine judges upon departure from the physical body.

"No god can wipe away sin merely through belief in them. Every single good and bad action will be weighed upon the scales. Faith without virtue offers no escape from the reckoning of deeds."
  • The Elysian Fields The realm of eternal reward, illumination, and peace reserved for those who lived virtuously and fulfilled their sacred duties.
  • The Asphodel Meadows The resting place for neutral souls who slipped or lived without great virtue or great malice. Here, memories fade gently as souls await their next cycle of reincarnation.
  • Tartarus The realm of purification where souls who cast aside virtue are held to cleanse their severe transgressions through restorative torment before any future rebirth.

Memories of past lives exist quietly within human consciousness. While another chance at rebirth is granted, devotees aspire to live righteously now to spare the soul needless suffering.

The Divine Nature & Sacred Alliances

Polytheism & Interpretatio

The universe is animated by countless divine beings. The gods of Rome, Greece, and Anatolia resonate deeply with our modern civilization—evidenced by the neoclassical temples that shelter our laws and governments in 2026 CE. The ancients understood that different cultures perceive the same divine powers through local names (such as Minerva with Celtic Sulis, Venus with Inanna, or Mercury with Odin). We welcome the continuous recognition of deities across ancient Baltic, Slavic, Celtic, and Hindu traditions.

The Allegory of Mithras & The Bull

The famous Mithraic tauroctony is not a literal slaughter, but a profound cosmic allegory. The bull represents uncontrolled, monolithic dominance seeking to usurp cosmic balance (the storm god attempting to displace Jupiter/Jove). Mithras, alongside Lady Diana (the Dog), Lady Athena (the Snake), Lady Isis (the Scorpion), and Mercury (the Crow carrying words to Sol), holds back the bull to restore balance and pluralism across the cosmos.

Rejections of Dogma & Human Deification

  • Rejection of Monotheism: Dogmatic claims that only one deity exists remain unproven and have historically justified the destruction of indigenous cultures. We respect ethical paths (such as Zoroastrianism, Sikhism, and Hetanism) but firmly uphold polytheism.
  • Rejection of Human Deification: Humans are not gods. We reject the Imperial Cult of declaring living rulers divine, the self-deification of cult leaders, or equating human teachers with cosmic architects. Ancestors are revered as Manes, never worshipped as deities.
  • Rejection of Magic & Superstition: We reject magical deceit, fraudulent spells, and supernatural tricks that violate truth and integrity.

Sacred Rituals & Bloodless Offerings

Bloodless Sacrifice

Following the Platonic and Pythagorean lineages, we strictly reject animal sacrifice and harm. The gods lack nothing and require no blood. True sacrifice is a gift given in gratitude and mutual respect. Proper offerings include:

  • Pure Liquids: Libations of wine, spring water, milk, and olive oil.
  • Vegetation & Grains: Spelt cakes (mola salsa), figs, grapes, bay laurel, and barley.
  • Incense & Aromatics: Frankincense, myrrh, lavender, and honey.

Prayer, Temples & Community Conduct

  • Times of Prayer: At sunrise, sunset, before meals, and during significant life events. Missed prayers due to helping others are excused without guilt.
  • Sacred Temples: Built according to classical architectural standards (ideally oriented toward the West so the celebrant faces East toward the rising dawn).
  • Peaceful Outreach: We never proselytize door-to-door. Devotion is demonstrated through community public service, temperate civic engagement, and academic distribution of literature.

Milestones, Sacred Attire & Identity

The Temple Symbol

Our universal identifier is the Greco-Roman Temple Façade (🏛)—an instantly recognizable pillar of order, law, beauty, and virtue across the globe.

Traditional Garb

Natural fabrics (linen, cotton, wool). White tunics for daily life; Toga Praetexta (white with crimson border) for priests; black garments reserved strictly for mourning.

Sacred Weddings

Conducted in outdoor amphitheatres or sanctified natural groves rather than enclosed temple interiors, invoking the Great Father and Mother to bless the union.

Cremation & Funerals

Cremation is the sole sacred sendoff. Ashes are scattered in nature, kept at the Lararium, or placed in a Columbarium. Photographs celebrate the life lived without displaying corpses.
